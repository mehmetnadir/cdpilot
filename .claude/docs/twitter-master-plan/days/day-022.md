# Day 022 — T8 Hot Take

## Topic
CAPTCHAs are a regressive tax on the web

## Tweet 1/5 — Hook
CAPTCHAs don't stop sophisticated bots. They stop legitimate automation, accessibility tools, and users on slow connections. We built a trillion-dollar industry around a test that costs $0.002 to bypass. Who exactly is this protecting?

## Tweet 2/5
The economics are brutal: a CAPTCHA-solving service charges $1-3 per 1000 solves. Enterprise scrapers budget this in as a line item. The only people who actually get blocked are developers running personal projects, accessibility tools, and researchers. The bots still get through.

## Tweet 3/5
Modern stealth (what cdpilot's adaptive escalation does) bypasses the challenge entirely by not triggering it. Per-host stealth memory, behavioral normalization, and cookies save/load mean clearance tokens replay across sessions. The CAPTCHA only fires once, if at all.

## Tweet 4/5
@grok what's your read on the accessibility impact of modern anti-bot CAPTCHAs? Curious if there's solid research on how often hCaptcha/Turnstile blocks legitimate AT users vs actual bots.

## Tweet 5/5 — Anchor
The web needs a better security model. Behavioral analysis, account reputation, rate limiting — any of these are more accurate than "click the buses." CAPTCHAs are friction theater.
